# Production decisions for a real project

This guide implements Dilan's production requirements: preserve her voice, explain genuine decisions, connect speech to visible material, and show a next task that can actually be completed. It is written for this workspace and is not a named teaching framework.

## Choose something the material can support

List the project facts and the files available now. Select a decision or observation that can be shown with those files. If the only material is a final design, discuss that design from today's perspective. Do not invent an earlier failed version, a client request or a revision history.

For the packaging example, the available material supports a discussion of a small label, the information on it and the surrounding illustration. Dilan must supply the actual reason for her choice.

## Capture the explanation before polishing it

Invite a short voice note or a few rough sentences. Find the point Dilan is making and preserve the phrases that sound like her. Ask for one missing reason when it would change the story. Do not replace a missing answer with a plausible personal memory.

A brief on-camera explanation can use notes. It does not require memorizing a full script or recording the entire piece in one take.

## Give each spoken point something to show

Pair the explanation with a specific file, design detail, demonstration or camera action. Write what will be visible and why it helps someone understand the decision. If no supporting material exists, identify what needs to be recorded instead of implying that it is already available.

Use a camera setup that Dilan can reproduce. Preserve her requested on-camera role. Offer voiceover as a choice when useful rather than silently substituting it.

## Check the sequence with rough material

Time the actual explanation, then adjust the scene estimates. Remove repetition and identify any design detail that is mentioned but never shown. Record the missing footage from that short list.

Assemble a complete rough version before adding optional styling. Caption legibility, clear speech and an understandable example take priority over decorative transitions. Choose sound or music for a specific function, with a suitable license, when it helps the piece.

## Adapt the idea to its destination

Keep the underlying project facts stable. Change the opening, level of explanation and order of visuals to suit the selected platform and audience. A professional article may explain the constraint and decision in more depth than a short visual post. A portfolio Pin needs a clear image and destination.

## Make the next task observable

For each task, name one action, its owner, its prerequisite and the condition that marks it done. Put the first task that can be completed now at the top. Label draft copy and missing rationale clearly; do not label planned filming as finished production.

Finish by checking whether the person using the plan knows what to record next and what the recording must demonstrate.
